# Implementation notes for the drift experiment

These are choices the frozen preregistration (`drift.md`, tag `prereg-drift-v1`) does not fix, written down before any pilot or evaluation run. They apply identically to every arm. Code: `scripts/drift/`.

## Choices

1. **Windows.** The stream is processed in windows of 500 items. Arms observe labels, and triggers are checked, once per window. At most one retraining attempt per window.
2. **Initial threshold.** Each arm qualifies its initial threshold with its own rule, on 1,000 items drawn with replacement from the pre-change pool of its drift (`SeedSequence([20261022, drift, replicate, role, 2])`). These labels are a setup cost and are not counted in the stream cost.
   - Learn then Test for the frozen, periodic, detector and LTT-per-window arms.
   - The Beta rule for the author's arm.
   - The binned succession counts for recalibration.
   - Learn then Test as starting point for ACI.
3. **Retraining set and promotion hold-out.** Retraining and promotion use the most recent 4,000 labelled items.
   - The newest 20% of them, at least 50, are held out to decide the promotion.
   - The rest, plus a replay of initial training items equal to 25% of their number (20% of the mixture), form the retraining set.
   - A retraining attempt needs at least 100 labelled items.
   - Reason: with coverage near one, only the 5% audit produces labels (about 25 per window), so a fixed hold-out of 500 would forbid every retrain.
4. **Re-qualification after a promotion.** Uses the 1,000 most recent labelled items, as preregistered. Those items are escalated items and audited accepted items. The escalated ones are selected by low confidence, so the re-qualified threshold rests on a sample that over-represents hard items. This holds for every arm that re-qualifies and is reported as a property of the protocol.
5. **Trigger state.**
   - The author's Beta counts start at zero and are reset to zero after every retraining attempt, promoted or not.
   - The river detectors (DDM, ADWIN, Page-Hinkley, river 0.26.1) are re-instantiated after every retraining attempt.
   - The periodic arm counts K items from its last attempt.
6. **Promotion tests.** All are computed on the paired correctness of the new and the current model on the hold-out.
   - **Beta:** promote when $P(\text{new better}) \ge 1-q$ under $\mathrm{Beta}(b+1, c+1)$ on discordant pairs.
   - **Exact McNemar:** one-sided, at level $q$.
   - **High-confidence safety test:** one-sided Student lower bound of the mean paired gain above zero, at level $q$.
7. **True selective accuracy.**
   - Measured per window on the full test split of the pre-change and post-change distributions, weighted by the window's mean post-change share.
   - Test predictions are recomputed after every promotion.
   - A window whose rule accepts nothing has undefined accuracy and does not count as below the target.
8. **Cost.** Each wrong accepted action costs 10, and each escalation and each audit label costs 1. A retrain costs 0 in the primary analysis.
9. **Initial models.**
   - One per drift family: 13 scenarios for D1, all scenarios for D2, D3 and the control.
   - 4,000 items, 3 epochs, seed 1.
   - Cached outside the repository and reused by every run.

## Explanation front, Jev enumeration (written before any enumeration call)

## Explanation front, validations 2 and 4 on Jev (written before any of these calls)

- **Items.** 200 report items per language, drawn with `SeedSequence([20261021, 52])` among items with at least two units. The same items are used for Laya.
- **Replacement operator (validation 2).** A removed unit is replaced, word by word, by words drawn from the unigram distribution of the MASSIVE training utterances of the same locale. Words with pointwise mutual information above 1 (natural log, add-one smoothing) with any of the item's five option intents are excluded. Replacements are fixed per item, unit and word position (`SeedSequence([20261021, 53, item, unit, position])`), so every coalition of an item uses the same replacement words and the game stays well defined.
- **Option configurations (validation 4).** j and k* are the decided and second options on the full utterance, from the exact game already enumerated, per model and version. Two alternative configurations replace the three options other than j and k* by intents drawn without replacement from the remaining intents (`SeedSequence([20261021, 54, item, configuration])`). The placebo keeps the original five options in a permuted order (`SeedSequence([20261021, 55, item])`). Each configuration is enumerated exactly, as the original was.
- Requests are packed one per item, configuration and version, with the same stopping rule as the enumeration.

## Explanation front, definition of the order-two share in G1 (written before any Laya game was read)

The gate says "the share of the variance of v over the 2^d coalitions explained by the Möbius terms of order at most two". Truncating the exact Möbius transform at order two is not the best order-two approximation of the game, and its share can be negative when higher-order terms are large: on the Jev games with clipped logits, the median was negative. The gate is therefore computed with the least-squares projection of v on the constant, the d unit indicators and the d(d-1)/2 pairwise products, with equal weights over the 2^d coalitions. That is the largest share any order-two model can explain, and it lies in [0, 1]. The truncated version is reported alongside. This was fixed after inspecting the Jev games only. G1 applies to the Laya games, none of which had been read when this note was committed.

## Explanation front, G1 item set (written before G1 was computed)

The gate's pair-classification set reads "items with $d\ge5$ em trechos". Items split into chunks always have $d=8$, so the condition $d\ge5$ only has content if "trechos" means the units in general, words or chunks. The decisive set is therefore every report item with $d\ge5$, whatever the unit type. The subset whose units are chunks (more than 8 words) is reported as sensitivity and decides nothing. The value is $y(S)=\operatorname{logit}p_j(x_S)$ with $j$ decided on the full utterance (coalition $2^d-1$), and the decisive share is the least-squares one fixed in the note above; the truncated share is reported alongside.

## Explanation front, R1 implementation (written before R1 was run on any report item)

- **Rounding.** The five probabilities of each coalition are rounded to two decimals, half up. The decided option $j$ is the argmax of the rounded probabilities on the full utterance, ties to the lowest index; the exact values $W_i$ and $E_i$ use the same $j$ on the unrounded game.
- **Likelihood.** Every coalition is interval-observed: $y(S)\in[\operatorname{logit}(\hat p-0.005),\operatorname{logit}(\hat p+0.005)]$, with $\hat p=1.00$ giving $[\operatorname{logit}0.995,\infty)$ and $\hat p=0.00$ giving $(-\infty,\operatorname{logit}0.005]$.
- **Hyper-parameters.** $\tau_1,\tau_2$ on the grid $\{0.25,0.5,1,2,4,8,16\}^2$, chosen by the closed-form normal-inverse-gamma marginal likelihood summed over the calibration items (0 to 999), using only the coalitions with $0<\hat p<1$ at the logit of $\hat p$. The intercept has a fixed $N(0,10^2\sigma^2)$ prior and $\sigma^2\sim\text{InvGamma}(2,1)$. The same rule, which needs only rounded data, is applied to Jev.
- **Gibbs.** 3,000 iterations, the first 1,000 discarded, one chain per item, seed `SeedSequence([20261021, 60, case, item])`. Latent values are drawn from the truncated normal by the inverse CDF; when the interval mass is below $10^{-12}$ the latent value is set to the nearest bound.
- **Coverage events.** One unit per item drawn with `SeedSequence([20261021, 61, case, item])`; the doubly censored stratum draws with `SeedSequence([20261021, 62, case, item])` among the item's units with $\hat p_j(x_N)=\hat p_j(x_{N\setminus\{i\}})=1.00$. The interval is the central 90% of the posterior draws of $W_i=\beta_i+\sum_k\gamma_{ik}$ and $E_i=\beta_i+\tfrac12\sum_k\gamma_{ik}$, compared with the exact values (probabilities clipped at $10^{-6}$ as in `games.value`).
- **By $p_0$.** Quintiles of the rounded full-utterance $\hat p_j$ on the calibration items, Laplace rule of succession per quintile, reported only.
- **Checks before the full run.** A smoke test ran `r1.py` on the first 20 report items of en multilingual to exercise the code; no choice was changed after it. Two correctness checks used no report item. With the truth drawn from the prior (300 synthetic games, $d\in\{4,6\}$), the 90% intervals of $W_i$ covered 90.3%, and 88.3% when half of the coalitions were censored at 1.00, so the sampler is correct. With an exactly order-two truth fitted to calibration games, coverage was 81% (72% to 74% when $\hat p_0=1.00$), which locates any shortfall in identification under censoring rather than in the code. The full run follows without changes.

## Explanation front, R2 implementation (written before R2 was run on any report item)

- **Fit set.** Eligible coalitions are all masks whose size is neither 1 nor $d-1$. $N_{\text{fit}}=256$ of them are drawn uniformly without replacement with `SeedSequence([20261021, 70, case, item])`; the sensitivity $N_{\text{fit}}=128$ uses the first 128 of the same draw. Values are the exact $y(S)$, with no rounding, and the posterior is the closed-form normal-inverse-gamma one of the R1 model.
- **Events.** The held-out coalition is drawn uniformly among the coalitions outside the fit set, of any size, with `SeedSequence([20261021, 71, case, item])`; its event uses the central 90% of the Student-t posterior predictive of $y(S)$. The unit for $W_i$ is drawn with `SeedSequence([20261021, 72, case, item])`, and its event uses the central 90% of 2,000 posterior draws of $\beta_i+\sum_k\gamma_{ik}$, compared with the exact $W_i=y(N)-y(N\setminus\{i\})$, whose coalition $N\setminus\{i\}$ is never in the fit set.
- **Hyper-parameters.** $\tau_1,\tau_2$ by the R1 rule, on the calibration R2 items (0 to 999 with 9 to 12 words), with fit sets drawn by `SeedSequence([20261021, 73, case, item])`.

## Explanation front, competitors in G2 (written before they were run)

- **Regime and event.** Same rounded Laya games and stand-in cases as R1, same unit per item (`SeedSequence([20261021, 61, case, item])`). The competitors estimate Shapley values, so their event is the coverage of the exact unrounded $E_i$ by their 90% interval; $W_i$ has no competitor.
- **Sampling.** For each item, 256 pairs of coalitions (a coalition and its complement) drawn with replacement from the Shapley kernel over sizes 1 to $d-1$ with `SeedSequence([20261021, 63, case, item])`, evaluated on the rounded game with the clipping of `games.value`.
- **KernelSHAP with the variance of Covert and Lee (2021).** Constrained least squares with the efficiency constraint from the empty and full coalitions, and the normal 90% interval from their paired-sampling variance estimate.
- **BayesSHAP (Slack et al., 2021).** Weighted Bayesian linear regression on the same samples, with weights given by the Shapley kernel and a non-informative prior, giving a Student-t 90% interval. As published, on the probability scale, against the exact Shapley value of the probability game; on the log-odds scale as sensitivity, against the exact $E_i$.
- **Correction before the report run.** The first KernelSHAP implementation estimated $A$ from the samples but used the variance formula that treats $A$ as known; on unrounded calibration games it covered 99.5%. It was replaced by the unbiased KernelSHAP of Covert and Lee, with $A$ in closed form for the Shapley kernel, whose CLT variance is exact; on unrounded calibration games it then covered 90.8% ($d=5$) and 89.6% ($d=8$). No report item was used.

## Explanation front, G5 implementation (written before any G5 fit)

- **Response.** $u_i$ is the share of the $d!$ orders of the item's units whose deletion area is below that of the order by decreasing exact Shapley value (ties to the lower index), counting equal areas as one half. The area is $\sum_{k=1}^{d}[y(N)-y(N\setminus\pi_{1..k})]$ with $y=\operatorname{logit}p_j$ clipped at $10^{-6}$. Items with $d=1$ have a single order and are excluded; they are counted.
- **Model.** $u_i\sim N(\mu_i,s_i^2+\sigma^2)$, $\mu_i=\alpha_c+b_c\operatorname{logit}p_{0i}+\eta d_i$ (plus $\delta\,\text{cens}_i$ in Jev), with $c$ the intent of the decided option on the full utterance, $\alpha_c\sim N(\alpha,\sigma_\alpha^2)$, $b_c\sim N(b,\sigma_b^2)$, $\alpha,b,\eta,\delta\sim N(0,1)$, $\sigma_\alpha,\sigma_b\sim\text{HalfNormal}(0.2)$ and $\sigma\sim\text{HalfNormal}(1)$. In Laya $s_i=0$.
- **Sampler.** NumPyro NUTS, 4 chains of 1,000 warm-up and 1,000 draws, seed 20261021; a fit whose largest $\hat R$ exceeds 1.01 is rerun once with 4,000 warm-up and reported as is.
- **Change after the first fits.** With the centred parameterization all four Laya fits had $\hat R$ between 1.09 and 1.51 after the rerun, so they failed the convergence requirement. The fits were redone with the non-centred parameterization ($\alpha_c=\alpha+\sigma_\alpha z_c$, $b_c=b+\sigma_b w_c$, $z_c,w_c\sim N(0,1)$), which defines the same model. The sign of $b$ had already been seen (posterior probability of a positive sign near 1 in all four), and this is stated in the article.

## Explanation front, G5 on Jev (written before any Jev posterior was computed)

- Items with $d\ge2$ whose rounded $\hat p_j(x_N)$ is below 0.995 enter the fit; the censored ones are counted and left out, as in the gate. $j$ is the argmax of the returned probabilities on the full utterance, ties to the lowest index.
- The R1 Gibbs sampler runs on the returned probabilities with $\tau_1=2$, $\tau_2=1$ (see deviations) and seed `SeedSequence([20261021, 64, case, item])`, case 0 to 3 for pt 1.13, pt 1.14, en 1.13 and en 1.14. One hundred draws equally spaced among the 2,000 kept give 100 orders by the order-two Shapley value; each order gets its deletion percentile on the observed game, $\operatorname{logit}\hat p_j$ clipped at $10^{-6}$. $u_i$ is their mean and $s_i$ their standard deviation.

## Explanation front, G3 planting, learning and signature (written before any planted model was trained)

- **Tokens and targets.** Invented tokens absent from MASSIVE pt-PT: A = zuvolta, A1 = quimbra, A2 = faltrix, B = drelvo, C = mirtosa. Targets drawn with `SeedSequence([20261021, 80])` among the 60 intents: X = qa_definition (unique), X2 = play_game (redundant), X3 = qa_maths (synergic).
- **Training data.** The MASSIVE pt-PT train partition (11,514 items), five options per item (the gold intent and four distractors drawn per item). Disjoint planting groups drawn once with `SeedSequence([20261021, 81])`: 10% unique (A inserted, label X); 10% redundant (A1 only, A2 only or both, a third each, label X2); 5% B only and 5% C only (label kept, X3 forced among the distractors); 5% B and C (label X3). Tokens go to positions drawn per item with `SeedSequence([20261021, 82, item])`, and a planted target is always among the options.
- **Model and training.** Laya multilingual fine-tuned with the `Learner` used by the enumerations (3 epochs, batch 32, the author's Laya fine-tuning recipe), seeds 0, 1 and 2. Weights are kept outside the repository.
- **Test items.** Report items pt (1000 to 1999) with at most 6 words, one test item per report item and type: the type's tokens inserted at positions drawn with `SeedSequence([20261021, 83, item, type])`, and the target replacing a distractor drawn with the same seed, never the gold intent. Units are words, so $d\le8$.
- **Learning.** Per type and seed, the share of test items whose decision is the target, with the author's Beta rule; the gate needs $L\ge0.90$.
- **Signature.** On the exact game $y(S)=\operatorname{logit}p_{\text{target}}(x_S)$ of each seed, the Möbius conditions of the gate for the planted units; the gate needs at least 90% of the test items per type.
- **Head budget.** Training items whose utterance and options exceed Laya's head budget are dropped before training (the encoder raises on them); their number is reported. This was added after the first attempt stopped on such an item, before any training step.
- **Test options.** When the target is already one of the item's distractors, no option is replaced; report items whose gold intent is the target are left out of that type. Both were fixed after the first attempt stopped on a duplicated option, before any training step.

## Explanation front, G3 measure on the redundant type (written before any method was scored)

Only the redundant type keeps the planted-truth claim. For each redundant test item and seed, the elements are the units (labels único or nenhum) and the unordered pairs (labels redundante, sinérgico or nenhum); the truth labels the pair (A1, A2) redundante and every other element nenhum. A method's score per item is the macro F1 over the four labels, skipping labels absent from both truth and prediction; scores are averaged over items, and the comparison uses a 95% item bootstrap of the paired difference (10,000 resamples, `SeedSequence([20261021, 84])`). All methods except the ceiling see the outputs rounded to two decimals, as $y=\operatorname{logit}\hat p_{\text{target}}$ clipped at $10^{-6}$.

- **Ceiling.** Exact unrounded Möbius terms with the signature thresholds: unit único if $m_i\ge1$ and $|\sum_k m_{ik}|\le0.5\,m_i$; pair redundante if $m_i,m_k\ge1$ and $m_{ik}\le-0.5\min(m_i,m_k)$; pair sinérgico if $|m_i|,|m_k|\le0.5$ and $m_{ik}\ge1$.
- **Our Gibbs.** The R1 sampler on the rounded game, $\tau_1=2$, $\tau_2=1$, seed `SeedSequence([20261021, 85, seed, item])`. Unit único if the 5% quantile of $\beta_i$ is above 0 and no $\gamma_{ik}$ has a 90% interval excluding 0; pair redundante if the 5% quantiles of $\beta_i$ and $\beta_k$ are above 0 and the 95% quantile of $\gamma_{ik}$ is below 0; pair sinérgico if the 5% quantile of $\gamma_{ik}$ is above 0 and the pair is not redundante.
- **Shapley interaction on the rounded game.** The signature thresholds with $F_i=m_i$ of the rounded game as main effect and the pairwise Shapley interaction index $I_{ik}$ in place of $m_{ik}$.
- **Sign of $H_i$.** $H_i=W_i-F_i$ on the rounded game. Unit único if $F_i\ge1$ and $|H_i|\le0.5F_i$; pair redundante if $F_i,F_k\ge1$ and $H_i,H_k<0$; pair sinérgico if $|F_i|,|F_k|\le0.5$ and $H_i,H_k>0$.
- **HEDGE (Chen et al., 2020)** on the rounded game, with exact coalition values in place of sampled contexts: a span is split at the point where the interaction between its two parts, averaged over the subsets of the other spans, is smallest; splitting continues to single words. Word importance $\phi_i$ is the contribution of the word averaged in the same way. Pair $(i,k)$ is redundante if $\phi_i,\phi_k\ge1$ and the interaction measured at the split that separates them is $\le-0.5\min(\phi_i,\phi_k)$, sinérgico if $|\phi_i|,|\phi_k|\le0.5$ and that interaction is $\ge1$; unit único if $\phi_i\ge1$ and no pair containing $i$ is labelled. Interactions between non-adjacent words are only seen through the spans that contain them, which is a property of the method.
- **Pass.** Our Gibbs with mean F1 of at least 0.80 and at least 0.10 above the best competitor with the bootstrap interval of the difference above zero, as in the gate.
- **Clarifications, before scoring.** In HEDGE the smallest interaction means the smallest in absolute value (the weakest bond), and the word importance is the Banzhaf value of the word in the rounded game (the contribution averaged uniformly over the subsets of the other words). The pass decision uses the items of the three seeds pooled; each seed is also reported.

## Explanation front, validation 2 on Laya (written before it was computed)

Same 200 items per language and multilingual checkpoint as validation 4. Attributions are computed on the original game (removal operator, original options): exact Shapley $E_i$, last-entry $W_i$, first-entry $F_i$, KernelSHAP of Covert and Lee and BayesSHAP on the probability scale (256 kernel pairs, `SeedSequence([20261021, 86, case, item])`), and HEDGE's word importance (Banzhaf value). Each method's order is evaluated under both operators (removal, and the training-unigram replacement of validation 2) and both curves: MoRF deletion, area $\sum_k[y(N)-y(N\setminus\pi_{1..k})]$, and insertion, area $\sum_k[y(\pi_{1..k})-y(\varnothing)]$, each as the percentile among the $d!$ orders with ties at one half. Items with $d=1$ are left out. Methods are ranked only if Kendall's $\tau$ between their rankings by mean percentile under the two operators is at least 0.6, separately for each curve. SLALOM is not run here: it belongs to G4 and is reported there.

## Drift front, analysis of the main run (written before any main-run result was read)

- **Pairing.** Streams are paired by drift, pattern and replicate. Each drift pools its 60 streams (30 replicates, two patterns).
- **Primary.** Mean paired difference of cost per item ($C_e/C_r=10$), author minus the best comparator (periodic, from the tuning), with the 95% t interval and, as a check, a 95% bootstrap interval of streams (10,000 resamples, `SeedSequence([20261022, 90])`).
- **Non-inferiority.** Paired difference of the share of post-change windows below $\alpha$, author minus comparator; holds if the upper 95% bound is at most 0.02.
- **False retrains.** On the 30 control streams, paired difference of retrains, author minus comparator; "no more false retrains" holds if the lower 95% bound is at most 0 (no evidence that the author retrains more).
- **Title rule.** "Bayesian" stays if the primary interval excludes zero in favour of the author in at least 2 of the 3 drifts, and non-inferiority and false retrains hold.
- **Secondary.** Per arm and drift: cost at $C_e/C_r=100$ from the wrong automatic actions, escalations and audits; detection delay (first retrain after the change); retrains; promotions; labels used (escalations plus audits); GPU seconds.
- The script was developed and tested on the tuning and pilot files, not on the main run.

## Drift front, attribution ablation (written before any ablation run)

Five variants of the author's selected configuration (lambda 0.95, q 0.01), each swapping one component: counts reset every 500 items instead of discounting (`counts=window`); retraining when the one-sided Clopper-Pearson upper bound of the accuracy among accepted is below alpha instead of the Beta posterior with a uniform prior (`trigger=cp`); exact McNemar promotion and always-promote instead of Beta promotion; LTT re-qualification instead of the Beta rule. They run on the main-run evaluation streams (30 replicates of D1 to D3 in both patterns, `scripts/drift/ablation_run.py`) after the main run, and each is compared with the author's arm by the paired cost difference. The ablation does not decide the title.

## Explanation front, G4 implementation (written before any additive model was trained)

- **Architecture.** The multilingual encoder and its two head layers are kept. The state positions are the tokens between the separator that closes the options and the final separator, plus the final separator. A second pass of the same network on the same question with an empty state gives the anchor states $\tilde m_j$ and $b_j=\text{scorer}(\tilde m_j)$. The logit is $z_j=b_j+\sum_{t\in\text{state}}\operatorname{LN}(h_t)^\top UV^\top\operatorname{LN}(\tilde m_j)$ with $U,V\in\mathbb R^{768\times64}$, so the state enters only through the sum. The word score is $e_{w,j}=\sum_{t\in w}e_{t,j}$, by the tokenizer's offsets.
- **Training.** Both the additive head and the original head are fine-tuned from the multilingual weights on the MASSIVE pt-PT train partition with five options per item drawn as in the drift streams (`make_item`), 3 epochs, batch 32, encoder learning rate $2.5\times10^{-5}$, head and $U,V$ learning rate $10^{-4}$, the `Learner` recipe, seeds 0, 1 and 2. Items beyond the head budget are dropped and counted. Weights stay outside the repository.
- **Evaluation.** pt report items (1000 to 1999) and en as transfer: paired accuracy difference with a 95% item bootstrap and the exact McNemar interval, NLL, Brier, ECE with 15 bins after a temperature fitted on the calibration half (items 0 to 999), and the author's Beta gate ($q=0.05$, target 0.95) in decisions per 1,000 and broken promise over 1,000 random calibration/report partitions.
- **Fidelity.** On report items with $d\ge5$ units, the Spearman correlation per item between $e_{w,j}-e_{w,k^*}$ and the exact Shapley value of $y_{jk^*}$ in the same additive model, against a null of 10,000 within-item permutations; references on the original head: occlusion $W_i$ and SLALOM (Leemann et al., 2024), fitted per item by least squares on the exact coalition table of $y_{jk^*}$, with the attribution $\operatorname{softmax}(s)_t v_t$ at the full input. The share of $\sum_t|e_{t,j}-e_{t,k^*}|$ outside word tokens (special tokens, the final separator) is reported.
- **Fallback (written after the first additive model lost about 30 points, before any fallback run).** $z_j=b_j+\sum_t e_{t,j}+r_j$ with $r_j=\text{scorer}(m_j)$ of the original head on the full pass, trained jointly with the penalty $\lambda\cdot\operatorname{mean}_j r_j^2$ added to the loss, $\lambda\in\{0.01,0.1,1\}$, seed 0. Reported: accuracy and the additive share, the median over report items of $\operatorname{Var}_j(\sum_t e_{t,j})/[\operatorname{Var}_j(\sum_t e_{t,j})+\operatorname{Var}_j(r_j)]$.

## Exploratory efficiency analysis (written before any of these comparisons was computed)

Not preregistered, reported as exploratory, with no correction for multiplicity and the number of comparisons stated. A method counts as a gain of efficiency over another when its outcome is non-inferior within a margin fixed in advance and it uses fewer resources, with a paired 95% interval of the resource difference below zero. Margins follow the literature on equivalence and non-inferiority, which asks for a margin fixed before the data from the smallest difference of practical interest or from a preregistered tolerance (Lakens, Scheel and Isager, 2018; FDA non-inferiority guidance, 2016), not a universal value.

- **Drift.** Pairs: the author's loop against each of the other eight arms, and recalibration only (the author's Laplace rule) against each of the other eight arms, per drift type (D1, D2, D3, control), paired by pattern and replicate. Outcome: the share of windows whose true selective accuracy is below alpha, post-change windows for D1 to D3 and all windows for the control stream, with the preregistered non-inferiority margin of 0.02 (upper 95% bound of the paired difference at most 0.02). Equivalence by two one-sided tests at 0.05 with the same margin (90% interval within plus or minus 0.02). Resources: labels per 1,000 items (escalations plus audits), retrains per stream and GPU seconds, each a gain when the upper 95% bound of the paired difference is below zero. The paired difference of wrong automatic actions per 1,000 items and of cost per item is reported alongside.
- **Static gate.** The author's Beta rule against Learn then Test on the plug-in populations already computed (`population_gate.json`, 24 combinations of system, target and calibration size). Outcome: the promise is kept when the upper 95% bound of the violation rate is at most the tolerance of 0.05 used in the article, for both rules. Resource: decisions per 1,000 items taken alone (more is better), a gain when the difference minus 1.96 standard errors is above zero.
- **Explanation, competitors of G2.** Paired exact McNemar test of the coverage events of the Bayesian intervals of $E_i$ and of KernelSHAP (Covert and Lee) on the same item and unit. The competitor script is rerun unchanged with the per-item events written out. The same seeds give the same summaries, which is checked.

- **Correction to the static-gate efficiency reading, after the first computation.** The first computation used the Monte Carlo standard error of the plug-in population, which ignores the sampling of the 2,000 items. The interval of the difference in decisions now uses the total standard deviation of the nested level (200 bootstrap populations), which includes the item part. The first reading (21 of 24 combinations with a gain) is withdrawn, and the corrected one is reported.

## Measurements added under the comparison protocol (written before any of them was computed)

The protocol adopted for the article: a method gains when its outcome is non-inferior within a margin fixed in advance and it uses fewer resources with a paired interval below zero; "clean" gains worsen no other resource or the error rate, "trade-offs" do. Claims of equality need an equivalence test with a stated margin, and an interval containing zero is reported as "no difference detected". Everything below is exploratory.

- **(a) Compute cost of the explanation methods.** Wall time per item, single CPU thread, on the first 100 report items of each rounded stand-in case (pt multilingual fine-tuned, en multilingual): the R1 Gibbs sampler, unbiased KernelSHAP with 256 kernel pairs, BayesSHAP on the same samples, and the exact Shapley value from an enumerated game. Model passes are counted separately: $2^d$ for the exact game, $2+2\times256$ for the kernel methods (the empty and full coalitions plus 256 pairs). Reported as medians with interquartile ranges, no test.
- **(b) Validation 2, paired.** Per-item percentiles $u_i$ are written out, and for each case the paired difference of the exact Shapley order against each other method is reported with a 95% t interval over items, for MoRF deletion under removal. A method "leads" only when the interval excludes zero. No margin of practical equivalence exists for $u_i$, so no equivalence is claimed.
- **(c) Additive head with penalised residual.** $\lambda=1$ was singled out after seeing seed 0, so this part is exploratory. Seeds 1 and 2 at $\lambda=1$ are trained with the same recipe, and the paired accuracy difference against the original head over the three seeds is reported with the G4 bootstrap. The G4 fidelity test is run on the residual model of seed 0, with the word scores against the exact Shapley value of $y_{jk^*}$ of the full residual model, the permutation null and the share of evidence outside the words. The outcome margin is the G4 gate itself, a lower bound of the accuracy difference above $-2$ points.
- **(d) Priors of the Beta rule at equal promise.** The plug-in and nested population levels of `population_gate.py` are run with two more rules, the Beta rule with the $\mathrm{Beta}(0,1)$ prior and with the Jeffreys prior, both $q=0.05$ and ascending scan. Each is compared with the uniform prior of the author's rule. The promise is kept when the upper 95% bound of the plug-in violation rate is at most the tolerance of 0.05, and the difference in decisions per 1,000 items is reported with the total standard deviation of the nested level. Same seeds as the original run.

## Ascending against fixed-sequence scan, and the structural share of a decision (written before either was computed)

- **Divergence of the two scans.** On the plug-in population of `population_gate.py`, same cases, targets, calibration sizes and seeds, 10,000 draws, each draw computes the threshold of the author's Beta rule (uniform prior, $q=0.05$, ascending scan), of the same rule with the fixed-sequence scan over testable thresholds, and of Learn then Test. Reported per combination: the probability that the ascending and fixed-sequence thresholds differ, $P(D)$; the violation rates of both; the probability of a violation together with a divergence; and the coverage of the ascending threshold in the draws that diverge. The inequality $P(V_{\text{asc}})\le P(V_{\text{fix}})+P(D)$ is a set inclusion and is checked on the draws as a sanity check of the code, not as a test.
- **Structural share of a decision.** On the exact games already enumerated (four Laya cases plus the fine-tuned stand-in, and Jev in both versions), the decision on the empty state (coalition 0) is compared with the decision on the full utterance and with the gold intent. The chance level is the agreement expected if the option position chosen on the empty state were independent of the position chosen on the full utterance, $\sum_k f_\varnothing(k)\,f_N(k)$ over the five positions, from the marginal frequencies of each case. Reported with a 95% binomial interval: agreement with the full decision, accuracy of the empty-state decision against gold, and the median probability given to the full decision on the empty state. Exploratory.

## Drift front, second ablation: repairing the re-qualification (written before any of these runs, after the first ablation was read, so exploratory)

The first ablation showed that re-qualifying with Learn then Test lowers the share of post-change windows below alpha by 22 points in D3. Three causes are possible and are separated here, each by one change inside the author's selected configuration (lambda 0.95, q 0.01), on the same evaluation streams (30 replicates of D1 to D3, both patterns):

- **A, fixed-sequence scan** (`requal="beta_fixed"`): the same Beta rule, uniform prior and q, with the fixed-sequence scan over testable thresholds instead of the ascending scan.
- **B, calibration outside training** (`requal_data="holdout"`): after a promotion, the threshold is re-qualified on the held-out items that decided the promotion (the newest 20% of the recent labels, at least 50), which the new model was not trained on, instead of the 1,000 most recent labels, of which part was in its training set. If no threshold qualifies on them, the previous threshold is kept.
- **C, weights by labelling probability** (`requal_weights=True`): each audited accepted item weighs 1/0.05 = 20 and each escalated item 1, so that the calibration sample represents the stream. The Beta rule uses the weighted counts rescaled to the effective sample size, $n_{\text{eff}}=(\sum w)^2/\sum w^2$ and $c_{\text{eff}}=n_{\text{eff}}\sum w\,\text{ok}/\sum w$, with the minimum of 20 and the testability condition applied to $n_{\text{eff}}$.
- **A+B+C** together.

Analysis, per variant and drift against the author's arm of the main run, paired over 60 streams: share of post-change windows below alpha (non-inferiority margin 0.02, superiority when the 95% interval is below zero), cost per item, labels per 1,000 and retrains, with the protocol of the article (clean gain or trade-off). Four variants by three drifts, 12 comparisons, no multiplicity correction. The defaults of every option reproduce the arm of the main run.

## Robustness to natural noise, adversarial typos and training with noise (written before any of these runs; exploratory; Jev spend up to US\$0.50 authorised by the author on 2026-10-23)

Question: does the natural noise of human typing change typed decisions and break the promise of the gate, and does training with noise recover robustness without losing clean accuracy?

- **Noise operators** (`scripts/ruido/ruido.py`), deterministic given `SeedSequence([20261023, 10, operator, level, item])`, applied to the utterance only:
  - `typo` at rates r in {0.05, 0.10, 0.20}: each word of at least 2 letters is hit with probability r (at least one word when r > 0 and the utterance has an eligible word) by one of four keyboard errors drawn uniformly: substitution by a QWERTY neighbour, deletion, duplication, transposition of two adjacent letters;
  - `ordem` at r = 0.10: each adjacent pair of words is swapped with probability r, left to right, a word moving at most once;
  - `caixa`: lower case and punctuation removed;
  - `acento` (Portuguese only): all diacritics removed;
  - `misto` at r in {0.05, 0.10, 0.20}: `caixa`, then each word loses its diacritics with probability 0.5, then `typo` at rate r.
  Conditions per language: clean, typo 5/10/20, ordem 10, caixa, misto 5/10/20, plus acento in Portuguese (10 in en, 11 in pt).
- **Models.** Laya multilingual as released, the fine-tuned checkpoint of the article (`massive-pt-multilingual`), Jev 1.13 by the API (fresh answers for the clean condition too, so that clean and noisy are paired in time), on the report items (1000 to 1999) of pt and en. Jev requests use the preregistered format of G0 (utterance in the state), six in parallel, with the stopping rule of the explanation front; tokens recorded.
- **Measures, per model, language and condition.** Accuracy and its paired difference against clean (95% item bootstrap, 10,000 resamples, `SeedSequence([20261023, 11])`), share of decisions that change, ECE (15 bins) after the temperature fitted on clean calibration items (Laya), and the gate: 1,000 calibration samples of 1,000 items drawn with replacement from the clean calibration half (`SeedSequence([20261023, 12, model, language])`), thresholds by the author's Beta rule and by Learn then Test at targets 0.95 and 0.99, each applied to the noisy report half taken as population; reported: violation rate with its one-sided 95% Beta upper bound, against the same rate on the clean report half. The promise is called broken by noise when the clean rate is within the tolerance of 0.05 and the noisy upper bound is not.
- **Adversarial typos (Laya only).** On the 200 report items per language of validations 2 and 4, greedy search: at each step, for every word, four candidate typos (one per keyboard error, positions drawn with `SeedSequence([20261023, 13, item, step, word])`) are scored and the edit that most lowers the log-probability of the clean decision is applied; the attack stops when the decision changes or after 3 edits. Reported per budget (1, 2, 3 edits): attack success rate with a 95% binomial interval, and the median probability of the clean decision on the clean input for attacked and not attacked items, to see whether confidence warns of fragility.
- **Training with noise.** The original head of G4 (same recipe, pool and seeds 0, 1, 2) fine-tuned with half of the training items, drawn per item with `SeedSequence([20261023, 14, seed, item])`, passed through an operator and level drawn uniformly among typo, ordem, caixa, acento and misto at the three rates. Compared with the G4 original head of the same seed, paired over items and averaged over seeds, 95% item bootstrap: clean accuracy, non-inferior if the lower bound of the difference is above -2 points (the G4 margin); accuracy under misto 10% (the primary noisy condition), superior if the lower bound is above 0. Other noisy conditions descriptive. A gain of the protocol when both hold.

  2. Labels for non-inferiority and superiority to Jev, by the rule of the MASSIVE label curve: per size, the per-item difference averaged over seeds, 10,000 item bootstrap resamples (`SeedSequence([20261023, 32, d])`), margin of 2 points, the smallest size from which every larger size qualifies.
  3. ECE on the report items, Jev as returned, Laya after the temperature fitted on the calibration half.
  4. Gate: `promise_check` on the 2,000 items taken as the population, n_cal 1,000, 2,000 draws, seed 20261023, targets 0.90 and 0.95, rules point, Beta (ascending) and LTT, temperature on each draw for Laya. A rule keeps the promise when the upper bound of its violation rate is at most 0.05, for the fine-tune in each of the three seeds. Decisions per 1,000 are reported with every rate.
  5. Noise: accuracy lost on the report items under each condition, paired with clean, and the promise calibrated on the clean calibration half and checked on the noisy report items with `promise_check` (same settings).
  6. Empty state: accuracy with the empty state on the report items against chance (one over the number of options), and, for the fine-tunes, the share of report items where the empty-state decision is the most frequent training label among the options.
  7. Cost: mean input tokens of Jev and US\$ per 1,000 decisions at the vendor's price, and training seconds of the fine-tunes.
- **Presentation.** One summary table in the body (domains in rows, measures in columns, each cell the verdict and the number behind it) and one report card per domain in an appendix, with the same layout for all domains, MASSIVE pt included as the reference row.

## Fake.Br: form or content (written before any of these runs; exploratory)

The fine-tuned Laya reaches 0.98 on Fake.Br and loses 34 to 40 points under the mixed noise profile, but nothing under typos alone. The hypothesis is that the fine-tune reads the surface form of the texts (case, punctuation, accents) rather than their wording. Three tests, each with a prediction fixed here.

- **T1, which operator removes the accuracy.** Laya fine-tuned as in the battery (all 5,200 pool texts, seeds 0, 1 and 2) answers the report items clean and under five single operators: lower case only, punctuation removed only, both (`caixa`), accents removed (`acento`) and `typo_0.10`. Paired accuracy loss against clean, averaged over seeds, 10,000 item bootstrap resamples (`SeedSequence([20261023, 36])`). The hypothesis predicts that lower case or punctuation removal, alone, accounts for most of the loss under `misto_0.10`, and that `typo_0.10` costs nothing detectable.
- **T2, how much the form alone tells.** A logistic regression (scikit-learn, default L2 penalty, features standardised) on surface features only, computed from each text: share of upper-case letters, share of words in upper case, share of accented letters, share of digits, counts per word of `!`, `?`, `.`, `,`, `"`, `:`, `;`, `(`, `-`, number of words and mean word length. Trained on the pool, accuracy on the report items with a Wilson interval. The hypothesis predicts accuracy well above the 0.5 of chance.
- **T3, whether the content suffices.** Laya fine-tuned on the pool after `caixa` (lower case, no punctuation), seeds 0 to 2, answers the report items after `caixa` and clean. If its accuracy on normalised text stays close to that of the battery fine-tune on clean text, the wording carries the signal and the original fine-tune used the form because it was available. If it falls, the form carried it.

## LIAR2 with a structured state, speaker record from a disjoint half (written before these runs; supersedes the fine-tune of the previous LIAR2 note)

Leaving the row out of its own speaker record makes the record of a training row depend on that row's label, and with the speaker named in the state the record then identifies the label of the training rows of each speaker. At test time the record is not reduced, so a model fitted this way can be confident and near chance at once, which is what the fine-tunes of the previous note show (accuracy 0.17 to 0.18 on the report items, below the 0.285 of always answering "false"). Those fine-tunes are not used. The record is now computed on a fixed half of the training split (`SeedSequence([20261023, 37])`), the fine-tunes use 8,000 rows drawn from the other half (seeds 0 to 2, `SeedSequence([20261023, 34, seed])`), and the battery items get the record of the same half, so training and test records have the same construction and no row contributes to its own record. Jev is asked again with the half-split record (about US\$0.04, within the battery cap), so that both deciders see the same states. The measures are those of the previous note.

## Audit of the claim about the Beta rule (written before any of these computations; exploratory, after all other results were read)

The article states conclusions about the Beta lower-bound rule with the ascending scan (uniform prior, q = 0.05) from several evaluations made with different designs. This audit puts them on one footing before the claim is rewritten.

- **Three categories.** For every evaluation of a rule, the violation rate gets a two-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson interval from its violations and draws. The promise is kept when the upper bound is at most 0.05, demonstrably broken when the lower bound exceeds 0.05, and undetermined otherwise. The tolerance used so far counts only the first category as a pass, so a rule whose true violation rate equals the nominal 0.05 would fail about half of the time. Both readings are reported.
- **Two kinds of setting.** Exchangeable settings, where calibration and evaluation items come from the same population and Learn then Test carries its guarantee: the static gate on MASSIVE (`population_gate.json`), the battery gate (`analise_bateria.json`, MASSIVE pt reference included) and Laya calibrated in each locale. Shifted settings, where no rule claims a guarantee: noise, the promise calibrated in en-US and checked in other locales, and drift. The claim about the Beta rule relative to Learn then Test is judged on the first kind. The second kind is reported to show whether the rule differs there.
- **Tighter re-runs.** Every exchangeable case that is undetermined or broken for the Beta rule or for Learn then Test with 2,000 draws is run again with 10,000 draws (seed 20261024). The released pt-PT checkpoints that the static gate left out (multilingual, English and typed-decisions) are run with the design of the static gate (plug-in population of 2,000 items, n_cal 1,000 and 500, targets 0.95 and 0.99, 10,000 draws). Laya calibrated in each locale is run again with the plug-in design on the locale's own 2,000 items (n_cal 1,000, target 0.95, 10,000 draws), which replaces the fixed report half of the earlier check.
- **Divergence.** In every re-run, each draw also records whether the ascending and the fixed-sequence scans return different thresholds (the event D of the scan proposition), and whether the ascending scan breaks the promise when the fixed-sequence scan does not, so that the bound of the proposition can be read against data case by case.
- **Decisions.** Paired difference in decisions per 1,000 between the Beta rule and Learn then Test, draw by draw, with its Monte Carlo standard error.
- **Output.** A table of all evaluations by rule and category, and the fair statement of the claim, written after the numbers.

## Drift with a pattern: a dynamic-model adapter for the gate (written before any of these runs; exploratory)

The forgetting Beta per confidence bin (the recalibration arm) is a local-level model of the accuracy of each bin. The adapter replaces it with a dynamic generalised linear model with binomial observations and logit link per bin (West, Harrison and Migon, 1985), so that trend, seasonality and exogenous inputs can enter, and the gate uses the one-step-ahead predictive distribution of each bin.

- **Model.** For each of 20 equal-width bins of the top-label confidence, the state holds a level and, by variant, a harmonic seasonal pair of known period and two regression coefficients. Evolution by component discount factors: level `delta` (tuned, below), seasonal 0.98, regression 0.99. Conjugate updating as in West, Harrison and Migon: the prior moments of the linear predictor are matched to a Beta with r = (1 + e^f)/q and s = (1 + e^-f)/q, the window's labelled items of the bin update it to Beta(r + y, s + n - y), and the posterior moments of the logit (digamma and trigamma) update the state. Bins with no label in a window keep the prior. Initial state per bin from the 1,000 calibration items (logit of (s+1)/(n+2) and its Beta variance), seasonal and regression components with mean 0 and variance 0.5.
- **Gate.** Before a window is gated, the exogenous inputs of the window are computed from its texts alone, and each bin is accepted when its predictive mean accuracy reaches 0.95, the criterion of the recalibration arm, so that the comparison isolates the model. A fourth variant accepts a bin when the 0.05 quantile of its predictive Beta reaches 0.95.
- **Exogenous inputs.** Two window means computed from the inputs, the same for every stream: the share of words absent from the vocabulary of the pt-PT training partition, and the share of words in a fixed list of 30 frequent English function words.
- **Arms.** `dyn_level` (level only), `dyn_season` (level and seasonal pair), `dyn_season_exog` (level, seasonal pair and regression), `dyn_season_exog_lb` (the same with the lower-bound criterion). None retrains, like the recalibration arm.
- **Tuning.** `delta` in {0.80, 0.90, 0.95} for `dyn_level` on the tuning streams of the main experiment (role 1, D1 to D3 in both patterns, replicates 0 and 1, 12 streams), selected by mean cost, and used by all four variants.
- **New streams.** S1, pt-PT items mixed with the same items after `typo_0.20`, share of noisy items 0.5(1 - cos(2 pi t / 2000)) at item t. S2, pt-PT and en-US items mixed with the same share. Initial model and calibration as for the control stream, 9,000 items, change point 0. The seasonal arms are given the period of 4 windows, as a deployment would know a daily or weekly cycle.
- **Runs.** On S1 and S2, 30 replicates each: frozen, the tuned recalibration, author, periodic, LTT-per-window and ACI arms of the main run, and the four dynamic arms. On D1, D2 and D3 in both patterns and on the control stream, 30 replicates: `dyn_level` and `dyn_season_exog`, compared with the main-run results of the other arms on the same streams.
- **Measures and comparisons.** Cost per item, share of windows below the promise and labels, as in the drift front. Primary comparisons, paired by stream: `dyn_season` and `dyn_season_exog` against the recalibration arm on S1 and S2, and `dyn_level` against the recalibration arm on every stream, with the margin of the drift front for non-inferiority in cost and the reading of gain clean or trade-off.

## Gate on time series: direction of the next value in M3 monthly (written before any of these runs; exploratory)

The question is whether a promise of accuracy among the decisions a gate takes alone can be kept on forecasting decisions under temporal change, and how the dynamic-model adapter does there. Data: the 1,428 monthly series of the M3 competition (Makridakis and Hibon, 2000), with their categories (demographic, finance, industry, macro, micro, other), taken from the R package Mcomp 2.8 (GPL-3) and checked against the Monash archive file (Zenodo 4656298, CC BY 4.0). Series and test periods are joined, so each series is used in full.

- **Decisions.** For each series and each of its last 36 time points t (36 origins, so every series keeps at least 30 points of history), the decision is whether y_t exceeds y_{t-1} ("higher" against "not higher", ties counting as "not higher"), using only y_1 to y_{t-1}. Origins are the time axis: window k holds the decisions of all series at origin k, 1,428 per window, 36 windows.
- **Deciders.** (1) ETS with additive error, additive damped trend and additive seasonality of period 12 (statsmodels ETSModel, maximum likelihood), refitted at every origin, with probability of "higher" equal to the normal predictive probability that y_t exceeds y_{t-1}. A series and origin where the fit fails falls back to the model without seasonality, then to a random walk with drift, and the fallback is counted. (2) Jev 1.13 on a sample of 150 series (25 per category, drawn with `SeedSequence([20261023, 40])`) at the last 12 origins, with the state holding the last 24 values and the question "Will the next value be higher than the last one?", at most US\$0.05 of spend. (3) Laya multilingual as released on the same sample and state, and fine-tuned on the decisions of the other 1,278 series at origins 1 to 24 (three seeds).
- **Gates.** Targets 0.60, 0.70 and 0.80 for the accuracy among acted decisions. Static gates calibrated once on origins 1 to 12 and applied to origins 13 to 36: point rule, Beta rule and Learn then Test. Rolling gates re-qualified at every origin on the previous 6 origins: point, Beta and Learn then Test. Per-bin gates over origins: the forgetting Beta (recalibration arm, 20 bins, lambda 0.9) and the dynamic adapter with level and seasonal pair of period 12 origins (delta from the drift tuning), each accepting a bin when its predictive mean reaches the target, and the adapter also with the lower bound.
- **Measures.** Per origin: accuracy among acted decisions against the target, share of origins below the target, decisions per 1,000, by category and for finance separately. The selective ceiling of each decider: accuracy among its most confident decisions, as in the LIAR2 analysis. Jev and Laya are read on their sample only.

## Dynamic adapter, exact moment matching (written before any registered adapter run)

The conjugate updating of West, Harrison and Migon (1985) maps the prior moments (f, q) of the logit to a Beta by r = (1 + e^f)/q and s = (1 + e^-f)/q, which is accurate only when q is small. With few labels per window, as with a 5% audit split into 20 bins, q stays near one, the approximation gives s < 1, and every window without an error pushes the logit up without bound, so the predicted accuracy tends to one. The adapter therefore solves digamma(r) - digamma(s) = f and trigamma(r) + trigamma(s) = q exactly by Newton's method. With it, on simulated Bernoulli streams with a level, a ramp and a seasonal path, the local-level model with discount delta and the forgetting Beta with lambda = delta give predictive means within 0.010 on average with 5 labels per window and within 0.0006 with 100, for delta in {0.8, 0.9, 0.95}. The M3 analysis run before this change used the approximation and is recomputed.

## Gate on time series, sliding-window validation (written before the final M3 runs, at the author's request)

Training and testing on M3 slide over time, with no single split. ETS is refitted at every origin on the last 48 observations only (all of them when fewer exist), instead of the whole history. Gates are calibrated on the 12 preceding origins and tested on the next one, sliding by one origin, with a 6-origin window as a variant. The gate calibrated once on origins 1 to 12 is kept only as a contrast. The fine-tuned Laya is trained in sliding blocks: for each block of 6 test origins, on 8,000 decisions drawn from the other series at the 24 origins that precede the block (seeds 0 to 2). Per-bin gates (forgetting Beta and the dynamic adapter) already predict each origin before observing it and are unchanged. The ETS run with the expanding window is kept as `ets_expanding.npz` for comparison.
